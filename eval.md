# Field Voice self-check gate

Run this after producing a rewrite, and before returning it. Answer each check with pass or fail. If any check fails, fix the draft and re-run this list. Perform the check inline as part of producing the output; do not require a separate reviewer step.

For Diagnose requests, verify that the response names each pattern found, quotes the exact line, and gives a short fix, without rewriting the draft, scoring it, or claiming to know whether an AI wrote it.

## Voice and edit discipline

1. Does the edit preserve the writer's point without adding claims, examples, stats, quotes, owners, dates, or opinions?
2. Does it preserve the writer's distinctive vocabulary, cadence, bluntness, humor, uncertainty, and level of polish?
3. Did it leave strong human sentences alone instead of rewriting them for consistency or making every paragraph equally tidy?
4. Is the amount of cutting proportional to the actual slop, with no aggressive compression that strips out character?
5. Does the draft lead with what the reader needs while keeping personal setup that adds context, tension, or character?
6. Are concrete facts protected rather than smoothed into generic importance?
7. Does the draft use active voice with a human subject where the actor matters, and direct verbs instead of weak verb phrases?
8. Were genuinely tangled sentences fixed while clear fragments, spoken cadence, and changes in pace remained intact?
9. Is the structure preserved unless it was hurting the piece, with any reorganization explained in the Changed note?

## Words and patterns

1. Are inflated verbs, hype adjectives, significance puffery, empty phrases, and empty adverbs removed unless quoted, preserved from source, or carrying real emphasis?
2. Are binary contrasts, negative parallelisms, forced rule-of-three, throat-clearing openers, and faux-insight setups removed?
3. Are copula avoidance, synonym cycling, false ranges, and vague attributions replaced with plain, direct wording or a named source?
4. Are generic conclusions and summary-recap endings cut so the piece ends on a concrete point, takeaway, or next action?
5. Is formatting slop removed: decorative emojis, unnecessary bold inline headers, bullets that should be prose, and headers over tiny sections?
6. Are em dashes used sparingly, and is sentence case used for headings and after colons unless grammar or a proper noun requires otherwise?

## Credibility and safety

1. If Source-Locked Credibility Mode was active, were no facts, names, dates, numbers, product names, citations, owners, or commitments added, removed, or altered?
2. Were provided citations preserved next to their claims, no new citations introduced, and unsupported claims flagged rather than strengthened?
3. Were load-bearing qualifiers preserved, and was no commitment introduced or strengthened?
4. Was private or sensitive content kept confidential, with no schedule, location, travel, or internal-only rationale exposed in outbound-facing text?

## Final read

1. Would the writer recognize the edited draft as their own voice?
2. Would the draft read as natural to a sharp colleague?
3. Does the draft avoid robotic symmetry, repeated sentence shapes, and stacked punchy fragments?
4. Does the output return the rewrite first, with a short Changed note only when helpful or requested?
5. For Diagnose requests, does the response name each pattern with a quoted line and a short fix, without rewriting, scoring, or claiming AI authorship?
